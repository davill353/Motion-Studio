Before you start

AI works best on the boring, repeated jobs

You don't need "an AI strategy". You need the questions you answer every day handled properly — at 9pm, on a Sunday, while you're busy with a customer.

Every automation in this guide follows three rules we use with every client:

1. Start with your most repeated task

Not the most impressive one. The one that eats the most of your week.

2. Ground the AI in your own information

Your prices, your menu, your opening hours. It should never make things up.

3. Keep a human in the loop

Anything involving money, health, complaints or emails to customers gets approved by a person first.

What you'll need

  • An automation tool such as n8n, Make or Zapier (free or low-cost plans exist).
  • An AI model account (for example OpenAI) — usually a few pounds a month at small-business volumes.
  • Your existing tools: WhatsApp Business, Google Sheets, Gmail, Google Calendar, Telegram.

Costs and results vary by business and usage. The examples in this guide are designs we build and test; they're not guarantees of specific savings.

Instant replies to enquiries

Your first-week checklist

  • Write down the 10 questions customers ask you most.
  • Put your prices, hours and FAQs in one Google Sheet or document.
  • Choose one automation from this guide.
  • Decide what the AI must always hand to a human.
  • Test it with friends or staff before customers see it.
  • After two weeks, look at what it answered — and improve the information it uses.

Stay safe and compliant

Five rules for using AI with customers in the UK

1. Be honest that it's automated

Tell customers when they're chatting with an assistant, and how to reach a person.

2. Protect personal data (UK GDPR)

Only collect what you need, keep it secure, and say in your privacy policy which tools process it.

3. No made-up facts or reviews

Ground answers in your own information. Never use AI to write fake reviews or testimonials — it's illegal in the UK.

4. Keep humans on money, health and complaints

Use approval steps for refunds, medical questions and anything sensitive.

5. Watch your advertising claims

No guaranteed results, and no prescription-medicine claims in ads (ASA / CAP Code).

This guide is general information, not legal advice. If you're unsure, check the ICO and ASA guidance for your sector.
